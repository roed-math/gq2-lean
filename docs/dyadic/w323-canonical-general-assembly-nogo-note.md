# W323: the canonical general assembly has a genuine parity obstruction

W323 proves that the W322 placement boundary is not merely an unfinished proof obligation.
If the canonical range selection chooses `.M0 2` and the residue degree is odd, then

`CanonicalPivotPlacementDefectBoundary B S`

holds.  The intrinsic exact-level pivot exists, but parity and the Hilbert/norm criterion rule
out its placement on the distinguished `C` coordinate of the canonical compact-M word.  The
result is uniform in the handle count and does not use a nilpotency-class calculation.

It follows formally that neither `HasSelectedRowResidualRN` nor `HasSelectedAssemblyRN` can
hold for that unchanged selected presentation.  The latter remains impossible even after a
complete `GenAsmCompatibleFieldInputs` packet has been supplied, so field-arithmetic progress
alone cannot repair the canonical target.

This does not disprove the intended general Galois-group theorem.  It proves that its general
M-family construction must use the already-available mobile pivot through a new normal form,
presentation, or selector, rather than insist on the false distinguished-`C` condition.  The
next general wave should therefore formulate a mobile-pivot selected presentation and reuse the
uniform analytic and compatible-assembly infrastructure behind W322.
