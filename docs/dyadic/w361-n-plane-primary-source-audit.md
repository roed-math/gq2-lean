# W361 — primary-source audit of the strong N-plane lift

Independent bounded audit, 2026-09-17. This note adds no Lean premise, axiom,
source change, or claim that the stronger historical stabilizer is needed by
the completed selected presentation. It supplements
`w361-n-plane-scaling-audit.md`.

## Result

None of the primary results examined below supplies the exact simultaneous
integral character action required by `NPlaneScalingHypothesis`. This is a
statement about the audited results, not a claim that no such theorem exists
or that the desired automorphism is impossible.

The two new algebraic modules are sound, genuinely uniform intermediate
results. They remove particular proposed obstructions but do not supply the
remaining group lift. In particular, neither unmarked Demushkin
classification nor a unitary module isometry can silently replace that lift.

## 1. Exact target and parameter scope

Write the repository's marked N group as

\[
D_N(\alpha,h)=\langle a,b,c,d,u_j,v_j\mid
a^{e}[a,b][c,d]\prod_{j<h}[u_j,v_j]=1\rangle_{\mathrm{pro}\text{-}2},
\qquad e=2+2^\alpha.
\]

Here `a=x0`, `b=x1`, `c=sigma`, `d=x2`. For the q=2 N frame under
discussion, **alpha is at least two**. Its abelianization has the torsion
coordinate of order two generated by `a`, and free coordinates
`b,c,d,u_j,v_j`. The separate alpha=1 case has torsion order four and must not
be folded into this q=2 argument.

In `MarkedCore/N.lean`, `NPlaneRealizes` requires one continuous
automorphism `Psi` that preserves `chiN` and, simultaneously for every
continuous character `f : D_N -> Z2`, satisfies

\[
f(\Psi c)=\kappa f(c),\qquad
f(\Psi d)=f(d),\qquad
f(\Psi g)=f(g)\quad(g=a,b,u_j,v_j).
\]

Equivalently, on the torsion-free abelianization its matrix is
`diag(1,kappa,1,I_handles)`. Torsion additions are invisible to these
characters. `nCoreMat` literally updates only the `c,d` slots; its remaining
rows are not existential choices.

The weak `NScalingHypothesis` does not fix the other rows and is already
proved by `nScalingHypothesis_of_sl2`. The exact existing SL2 action supplies
`diag(kappa,kappa^-1)` on `c,d`, not `diag(kappa,1)`. A correction chosen
after one character is given has a different quantifier order from the
strong target. Orientation alone is not the missing constraint: the
completed `nCoreOrientationCharacteristic_all` already makes it
characteristic in the valid range.

## 2. Primary results checked

### Labute: the relevant classification proof does not pin the free rows

Theorem 2 gives equivalence of Demushkin relators with matching orientation
image by an automorphism of the free pro-p group. Theorem 5 is the pertinent
even-rank q=2 normal form. In its proof, `X=ker(chi)` and the corrections
belong to `X_i=X intersect F_i`, where `F_i` is the descending 2-central
series. The initial and limiting bases agree modulo `X_2`; no prescribed
integral abelianization matrix is concluded. Lemma 4 controls the graded
correction image through crossed derivations, not through simultaneous
vanishing of all ordinary Z2 characters. [J. P. Labute, *Classification of
Demushkin Groups*, Theorems 2 and 5 and section 4.1, especially printed
pp. 123–126](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/3045C3866BBC24C718D94C18F8F68E58/S0008414X00054158a.pdf/classification-of-demushkin-groups.pdf).

There is a direct local test of the missing inference. In our N marking,
`chi(c)=1`, hence `c^2` lies in the orientation kernel and in the second
2-central term. But the coordinate character taking `c` to 1 and all other
free generators to 0 takes `c^2` to 2, not 0. Thus the allowed correction
subgroup is strictly larger than the common ordinary-character kernel.
Convergence of increasingly deep corrections does not force their total
ordinary-character value to vanish. This identifies a missing proof step;
it does not show that better choices of corrections cannot exist.

### Wilkes: a pair classification is not an integral-row realization theorem

Theorem 3.3 classifies free pro-p PD2 pairs by the existence of a standard
form basis for a chosen peripheral generator. It does include q=2. However,
its q=2 proof changes the free generators by higher-filtration factors and
also adjusts peripheral powers; the final step uses Demushkin
classification. The theorem prescribes neither the full initial integral
character matrix nor identity on every complementary free row. It is
therefore not the needed relative IA lifting statement. [G. Wilkes,
*Classification of pro-p PD2 pairs and the pro-p curve complex*, Theorem
3.3 and its q=2 proof, PDF pp. 15–19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.02953).

### A close peripheral scaling theorem has the wrong prime hypothesis

Proposition 4.7(2) constructs an automorphism of a free pro-p group on
`x,y,s1` preserving `s1` up to conjugacy and scaling the other boundary
`s1 x^(p^r) [x,y]`, for a congruence-restricted unit exponent. Section 1.2
fixes p to be odd throughout; its proof also explicitly uses oddness.
Moreover, the conclusion does not prescribe the integral `x,y` rows.
It therefore does not instantiate our pro-two N target. [O. Ben-Bassat
and N. Gropper, *Arithmetic field theory via pro-p duality groups*, section
1.2 and Proposition 4.7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11005-026-02058-8).

### Refinements that still stop at a finite character quotient

Proposition 2.12 starts with a suitable relator congruence and produces a
normal-form basis congruent to the original basis modulo `F_2`. In its
q-central filtration, q=2 makes `F_2` the Frattini subgroup. The preceding
symplectic module is over `R=Z_p/q Z_p`, which here is F2. Corollary 2.13
controls an isotropic space of residue-field characters, not all integral
characters. These precise conclusions are compatible with the desired
lift but do not prove it. [M. Shusterman and P. Zalesskii, *Virtual
retraction and Howson's theorem in pro-p groups*, section 2.2.2,
Propositions 2.11–2.12 and Corollary 2.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.09096).

Two further promising-looking references have explicit scope barriers:

- Jannsen–Wingberg's adapted-generators theorem is for an elementary
  abelian p-extension and explicitly assumes `q != 2`. It does not give
  this q=2 integral-row automorphism. [*Demushkin-Erzeugende einer
  elementar-abelschen p-Erweiterung*, Satz 1, printed p. 30](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/26686/1/jannsen15.pdf).
- Bockle's group-action section assumes `p > 2` and a finite acting group
  of order prime to p. Its quoted lifting criterion already assumes that
  the Frattini action lies in the image of the group-automorphism action.
  It cannot be used to produce the missing integral pro-two action.
  [*Demushkin Groups with Group Actions and Applications to Deformations
  of Galois Representations*, section 3, printed p. 117 and Lemma
  3.1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/5CD1BF2F5CA81FF2BBD884BB919A7E3C/S0010437X0000018Xa.pdf/div-class-title-demuskin-groups-with-group-actions-and-applications-to-deformations-of-galois-representations-div.pdf).

Search results reproducing this project's own presentation or paraphrasing
its marked-automorphism claims were not counted as independent support.

## 3. Independent read-through of the new algebraic modules

### `NPlaneCentralCorrection.lean`

The arithmetic equation is exact:

\[
s e+\kappa=1.
\]

At alpha at least two, `e=2*(1+2^(alpha-1))` is twice a unit, so every odd
unit `kappa` admits such an `s`. In any compact Hausdorff pro-two group
where `z=[c,d]` is central in the **whole ambient group**, the substitution

\[
a'=z^s a,\qquad c'=c^\kappa
\]

leaves `a^e[a,b][c,d]` literally unchanged. The proof correctly uses
centrality both for powers of `z^s a` and for `[c^kappa,d]=z^kappa`.
Multiplication by any unchanged handle tail preserves the equality. All
ordinary characters kill `z^s`, so the displayed substitution also has the
claimed simultaneous character values.

This disproves a putative obstruction coming solely from the central
commutator test. It does **not** prove that `[c,d]` is central in `D_N`, or
that this substitution defines an automorphism of `D_N`. The file's
statements and introductory disclaimer make both limitations explicit.
The alpha restriction is essential to the uniform divisibility argument.

### `NPlaneUnitaryShadow.lean`

The coefficient ring is the concrete fiber product

\[
S=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z_2^2:x\equiv y\pmod2\},
\qquad (x,y)^*=(y,x).
\]

Its exhibited unit is `u=(1,kappa)`, with inverse `(1,kappa^-1)`. The
linear plane map is

\[
(c,d)\longmapsto(u^*c,u^{-1}d).
\]

It preserves `c^* d' - d^* c'` exactly; its inverse is the same formula
with `kappa^-1`. The first evaluation has matrix `diag(kappa,1)` and the
second `diag(1,kappa^-1)`. Taking its product with the identity on every
handle preserves the full displayed skew pairing at every rank.

The orientation interpolation also checks: for
`v=-(1+2^alpha)^-1`, one has `v-1=-e/(1+2^alpha)`, so the central
correction exponent supplies the affine unit taking 1 to 1 and v to
kappa. The proof separately verifies that this affine expression is a
unit at every odd argument.

These are constructed units and isometries, not assumed ones. However,
the file does not identify S with the actual N Alexander module or
identify its form with the full group-theoretic boundary invariant. It
does not assert a lift to `Aut(D_N)`. No error or hidden group-lifting
assumption was found in this read-through.

## 4. Exact remaining general step

Let

\[
K_{\rm ord}=\bigcap_{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm cont}(D_N,\mathbb Z_2)}\ker f.
\]

This is the inverse image of the torsion subgroup of the abelianization.
To correct a tentative generator tuple without changing its required
rows, corrections must be controlled in `K_ord`, not merely in
`ker(chi)`. A sufficient route is a relative relator-correction theorem
which preserves those rows and the full N relation while retaining
Frattini generation. The existing pro-two presentation and Hopfian
machinery can then promote the resulting endomorphism to an
automorphism. The relator correction itself is the substantive missing
step, not that promotion.

Alternatively, a free-group automorphism preserving the actual compact
N boundary word **as a word**, with the desired ordinary rows, can be
extended by identity on an arbitrary handle tail. An automorphism only
of the compact quotient does not suffice: in the positive-handle group
the compact word equals the inverse handle product, not 1. A completed
coefficient-ring calculation must therefore be linked to an actual
relative group or HNN-boundary lift before it closes the target.

No class-by-class computation is proposed here. Neither of the two
checked shadows creates a no-go theorem, and neither bypasses this
uniform lifting step. The completed selected presentation remains
independent of the stronger historical hypothesis.
